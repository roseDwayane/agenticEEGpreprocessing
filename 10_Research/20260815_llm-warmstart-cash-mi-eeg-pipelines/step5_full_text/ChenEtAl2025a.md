## Abstract

The efficacy of machine learning across scientific and industrial domains hinges critically on hyperparameter configuration, a process that dominates model performance yet remains computationally prohibitive. Conventional optimization strategies, such as Bayesian methods, treat models as black boxes; they ignore the semantic context of the data, necessitating expensive cold-starts and exhaustive search to identify viable parameters. Herein, we present HALO (Hyperparameter Auto-tuning via LLM Optimizer), a framework that integrates the semantic reasoning of Large Language Models (LLMs) directly into the optimization loop to transform tuning into an interpretable, knowledge-driven process. By orchestrating a dual-agent system, HALO leverages dataset semantics to formulate informed warm-start configurations and iteratively refines values through natural-language reasoning. On the high-dimensional SECOM industrial dataset, HALO achieves the highest Area Under the ROC Curve (AUC) in just four trials, significantly outperforming established advanced methods. These results demonstrate that infusing optimization with semantic understanding not only drastically reduces computational overhead but also transforms hyperparameter tuning into a transparent, interpretable process.

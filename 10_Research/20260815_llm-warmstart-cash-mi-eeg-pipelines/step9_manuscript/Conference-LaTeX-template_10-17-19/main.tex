\begin{abstract}
Subject-specific EEG motor-imagery (MI) decoding pipelines outperform one-size-fits-all designs, but optimizing the full preprocessing--decoding pipeline for every new user is prohibitively expensive: each optimization restarts from scratch, and automated hyperparameter search can take hours per subject. We formulate subject-specific pipeline design as a transfer variant of the combined algorithm selection and hyperparameter optimization (CASH) problem, treating each subject as a task. A decomposition-based CASH engine in the VolcanoML/SMAC lineage is extended with two interchangeable knowledge sources injected through a similarity-gated, $\pi$BO-style prior: (a) leave-one-subject-out search histories from other subjects and (b) priors elicited from a large language model. Evaluation follows a budget-matched anytime protocol with random-search, seeded cold-start, and fixed standard-pipeline controls, addressing known deflationary confounds of AutoML and LLM-advisor evaluations. In a pilot on PhysioNet EEGMMI, warm-started search reached a balanced accuracy of 0.950 versus 0.915 for cold-started search under the same budget, and zero-shot transfer of a source subject's configuration retained a substantial fraction of the searched improvement (0.765 vs.\ a 0.635 fixed-pipeline baseline) without any target-subject search. These results outline a path from per-user cold-start search toward calibration-efficient, subject-specific BCI pipelines.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Motor-imagery brain--computer interfaces decode movement intention from EEG through a multi-stage pipeline of temporal filtering, epoching, spatial filtering, and classification \cite{CraikEtAl2019, SchirrmeisterEtAl2017}. Pipeline performance is strongly subject-dependent: EEG feature distributions vary across individuals by up to an order of magnitude on common features \cite{ZhangEtAl2024}, and subject-specific configurations consistently outperform fixed designs \cite{AnarakiEtAl2024, WangEtAl2026}. In practice, however, most studies freeze the preprocessing chain to community conventions---e.g., an 8--30\,Hz band-pass filter with common spatial patterns (CSP) and linear discriminant analysis---and tune only the classifier \cite{CraikEtAl2019, LeonEtAl2020}. Optimizing the whole pipeline per subject is rarely attempted because it is expensive: automated hyperparameter optimization for a single BCI setting has been reported to take on the order of twenty-four hours \cite{BerdyshevEtAl2024}. This per-user search cost is the computational face of the BCI calibration problem.

Machine learning research has formalized exactly this setting. The CASH problem treats algorithm choices and hyperparameters across pipeline stages as one hierarchical search space \cite{KotthoffEtAl2019, HutterEtAl2019}, solved by Bayesian optimization (BO) engines such as SMAC \cite{LindauerEtAl2021} and, for large conditional spaces, by decomposition-based systems whose advantage over monolithic BO grows with dimensionality \cite{LiEtAl2021b, LiEtAl2020a}. In parallel, transfer hyperparameter optimization reuses knowledge from past tasks: meta-feature-matched warm-starting of the initial design \cite{FeurerEtAl2015}, and prior injection at the acquisition function, where $\pi$BO achieves up to $12.5\times$ faster time-to-accuracy from a simple default-centered prior while provably recovering from wrong priors \cite{HvarfnerEtAl2022}. Most recently, large language models (LLMs) have entered the loop as warm-starters and prior sources \cite{LiuEtAl2024, XuEtAl2026a}, with independent reproductions confirming the warm-start effect at 70B-parameter scale \cite{RychertEtAl2025}---and budget-matched audits warning that some claimed LLM gains reduce to a single good default configuration \cite{RodriguesEtAl2026}.

Despite this methodological supply and the evident application demand, the intersection is empty. Across 124 studies screened in our systematic review, no work injects transferable knowledge---search histories from other subjects or LLM-elicited priors---into BO-based CASH over a full EEG pipeline. EEG pipeline automation relies on cold-start evolutionary search \cite{NakisaEtAl2018, ZhuEtAl2025}, and the cross-subject transfer literature moves model weights and meta-learned initializations \cite{BerdyshevEtAl2024, AristimunhaEtAl2025} but never configuration knowledge; per-subject optimization, where attempted, restarts cold each time \cite{ZhangEtAl2024}. Meanwhile the CASH systems literature names meta-learned warm-starting as its own next step \cite{LiEtAl2016, LiEtAl2020a, LiEtAl2022a}, and LLM-agent BCI tooling explicitly calls for LLM-driven pipeline optimization \cite{KapitonovaBall2024}.

We close this gap by treating each EEG subject as a task in the transfer-HPO sense. Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
    \item a \emph{subjects-as-tasks} formulation of subject-specific EEG pipeline optimization as transfer-CASH, bridging decomposition-based AutoML \cite{LiEtAl2021b} and cross-subject BCI adaptation \cite{BerdyshevEtAl2024};
    \item a dual knowledge-injection design in which cross-subject search-history priors and LLM-elicited priors enter one similarity-gated $\pi$BO-style mechanism \cite{HvarfnerEtAl2022}, enabling like-for-like comparison;
    \item a deflation-proof, budget-matched anytime evaluation protocol with random-search, seeded cold-start, and fixed-pipeline controls \cite{ZollerHuber2019, RodriguesEtAl2026};
    \item pilot evidence on PhysioNet EEGMMI that warm-starting improves best-in-budget accuracy (0.950 vs.\ 0.915) and that zero-shot configuration transfer is viable (0.765 vs.\ 0.635 baseline).
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\textbf{CASH and AutoML systems.} Auto-WEKA introduced CASH \cite{KotthoffEtAl2019}; successors include TPOT's evolutionary pipelines \cite{OlsonEtAl2016}, AutoGluon's stacking \cite{EricksonEtAl2020}, and engines such as SMAC3 \cite{LindauerEtAl2021} and Optuna \cite{AkibaEtAl2019}. VolcanoML decomposes CASH into tree-structured execution plans that scale better than monolithic BO \cite{LiEtAl2021b, LiEtAl2022a}; Rising Bandits eliminate weak algorithm arms with $\sim$12.6$\times$ fewer trials \cite{LiEtAl2020a}. Benchmark studies caution that optimizer differences can vanish under rigorous protocols \cite{ZollerHuber2019} and name anytime reporting and transfer-HPO task families as open problems \cite{GijsbersEtAl2019, EggenspergerEtAl2021}.

\textbf{Warm-starting and transfer BO.} Knowledge can enter at the initial design \cite{FeurerEtAl2015}, the surrogate \cite{WistubaGrabocka2021, LiEtAl2022c}, the search space \cite{LiEtAl2022d}, or the acquisition prior \cite{HvarfnerEtAl2022}. Surrogate-level transfer is structurally fragile under task heterogeneity \cite{HvarfnerEtAl2026}; similarity-gated warm-starting with cold-start fallback provides a guard \cite{NomuraEtAl2021}. Given documented inter-subject variability \cite{ZhangEtAl2024}, we inject only at the initial-design and acquisition-prior levels, gated by subject meta-features.

\textbf{LLMs in the loop.} LLAMBO demonstrated LLM warm-starting as the most robust of its components \cite{LiuEtAl2024}; hybrid designs condition classical BO on LLM-derived context \cite{XuEtAl2026a}. Reproductions require $\geq$70B backbones \cite{RychertEtAl2025}, and budget-matched audits show gains can reduce to a good first configuration, with seeded classical search overtaking within twelve evaluations \cite{RodriguesEtAl2026}. We adopt these constraints as design choices (API-scale backbones, fixed prompts, seeded control).

\textbf{Automated and subject-adaptive EEG decoding.} Existing EEG automation is dominated by evolutionary/swarm search over architectures \cite{NakisaEtAl2018, DuanEtAl2023, ZhuEtAl2025, WangEtAl2026}; the statistical significance of HPO effects in deep EEG decoding remains largely untested \cite{CooneyEtAl2020}. Cross-subject work transfers weights and initializations---EEG-Reptile automates meta-learning with a few-shot ceiling far below subject-specific training \cite{BerdyshevEtAl2024, SchirrmeisterEtAl2017}---while the closest configuration-level precedents are classifier recommendation from dataset characteristics \cite{AnarakiEtAl2024} and cross-subject channel selection \cite{WeiEtAl2023}. Unlike these, we transfer \emph{search knowledge} over the full pipeline space and let a decomposed CASH search continue adapting to the target subject.

\section{Methodology}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Search Space and Engine}
We define a five-stage conditional pipeline space for MI decoding: resampling; band-pass filtering (FIR/IIR with low/high cutoffs); re-referencing; epoching window; CSP spatial filtering (number of components); and classifier selection among LDA, SVM, random forest, logistic regression, and QDA with their hyperparameters. The space is searched by a decomposition-based CASH engine in the VolcanoML lineage \cite{LiEtAl2021b}: a tree-structured execution plan conditions on the classifier choice, with SMAC-style BO \cite{LindauerEtAl2021} in each leaf subspace.

\subsection{Knowledge Sources and Injection}
Two knowledge sources are constructed per target subject $s$:
\emph{(a) Search-history priors.} Under leave-one-subject-out (LOSO) separation, the search histories of the remaining $N{-}1$ subjects yield per-hyperparameter priors and a ranked list of top configurations \cite{FeurerEtAl2015, BaiEtAl2023}.
\emph{(b) LLM-elicited priors.} An API-scale LLM, prompted with dataset and subject descriptors under a fixed released template, returns distributions over pipeline choices and hyperparameters \cite{LiuEtAl2024}.
Both sources are injected identically: top-$k$ configurations seed the initial design, and the prior multiplies the acquisition function with an iteration-decaying exponent, following $\pi$BO \cite{HvarfnerEtAl2022}, which guarantees recovery from misleading priors. A similarity gate $g(s)\in[0,1]$, computed from subject meta-features (band power statistics, CSP eigen-spectrum, baseline accuracy), attenuates the prior toward uniform when the target subject lies far from the source pool \cite{NomuraEtAl2021}, guarding against negative transfer for outlier subjects \cite{ZhangEtAl2024, HvarfnerEtAl2026}.

\subsection{Zero-Shot Configuration Transfer}
As a search-free operating point, the gated prior's single best configuration is applied directly to the target subject (budget $B{=}0$). This quantifies how much of the searched improvement survives without any target-subject evaluations---a quantity, to our knowledge, unmeasured at the configuration level in EEG.

\section{Experimental Protocol}
\label{sec:experiment}

\textbf{Data.} PhysioNet EEG Motor Movement/Imagery (109 subjects) as the primary corpus; BCI Competition IV-2a for external validation. Within-subject evaluation uses 5-fold cross-validated balanced accuracy; prior learning is strictly LOSO to avoid meta-learning contamination \cite{EggenspergerEtAl2021}.

\textbf{Conditions.} (1) fixed standard pipeline (8--30\,Hz FIR + CSP + LDA); (2) random search; (3) cold-start decomposed CASH; (4) \emph{seeded} cold-start, receiving the prior's best configuration as its first evaluation---isolating first-configuration quality from prior-shape guidance \cite{RodriguesEtAl2026}; (5) history-prior warm-start; (6) LLM-prior warm-start; (7) zero-shot transfer ($B{=}0$).

\textbf{Endpoints.} All searched conditions consume identical budgets $B\in\{25,50,100\}$. Primary endpoints are the area under the incumbent balanced-accuracy curve (anytime AUC) and evaluations-to-target \cite{GijsbersEtAl2019}; secondary endpoints are fixed-budget final accuracy, zero-shot retention, and the negative-transfer rate with vs.\ without gating. Hypotheses are tested with Wilcoxon signed-rank tests across subjects with Holm--Bonferroni correction.

\section{Preliminary Results}
\label{sec:results}

Table~\ref{tab:pilot} reports a pilot on two EEGMMI subjects (5-fold CV, budget ${\sim}30$ evaluations; H1--H3 notation as above). Cold-start decomposed CASH already improves substantially over the fixed standard pipeline (S001: 0.775 vs.\ 0.555; S002: 0.915 vs.\ 0.635), confirming that the ``standard'' configuration is far from subject-optimal. Warm-starting S002's search with S001-derived knowledge raised best-in-budget balanced accuracy to 0.950 and reached the cold-start optimum in roughly half the evaluations, while zero-shot application of the transferred configuration---no target-subject search at all---retained 0.765, recovering approximately 41\% of the searched improvement over the baseline. These pilot numbers motivate but do not yet establish H1--H2; the full 109-subject protocol with seeded controls (H3) is ongoing.

\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We formulated subject-specific EEG MI pipeline design as a transfer-CASH problem, proposed a dual knowledge-injection framework with similarity gating, and specified a deflation-proof evaluation protocol. Pilot results indicate that cross-subject knowledge substantially accelerates per-subject pipeline search and that zero-shot configuration transfer is a viable low-cost operating point. Ongoing work scales the protocol to 109 subjects, adds the LLM-prior arm under fixed prompts, and releases the per-subject search histories as a realistic transfer-HPO benchmark for the AutoML community \cite{EggenspergerEtAl2021}.

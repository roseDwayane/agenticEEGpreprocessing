\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

% --- 1. Context & Motivation (~400 words) ---

Motor-imagery brain--computer interfaces (MI-BCIs) decode movement intention from electroencephalography (EEG) through a multi-stage pipeline of temporal filtering, epoching, spatial filtering, and classification \cite{CraikEtAl2019, SchirrmeisterEtAl2017}. The performance of this pipeline is notoriously subject-dependent: EEG feature distributions vary substantially across individuals, with outlier subjects deviating from group statistics by more than an order of magnitude on common features \cite{ZhangEtAl2024}. As a consequence, a configuration that decodes one user well may perform poorly for the next, and pipelines tuned per subject consistently outperform one-size-fits-all designs \cite{AnarakiEtAl2024, WangEtAl2026}. Yet in practice, most BCI studies fix the preprocessing chain to community conventions---for example, an 8--30\,Hz band-pass filter followed by common spatial patterns (CSP) and linear discriminant analysis---and concentrate optimization effort exclusively on the classifier or the neural architecture \cite{CraikEtAl2019, LeonEtAl2020}. Whether the ``standard'' pipeline is anywhere near optimal for a given subject is rarely asked, and answering it by brute force is expensive: automated hyperparameter optimization for a single BCI setting has been reported to take on the order of twenty-four hours \cite{BerdyshevEtAl2024}. This per-user optimization cost is the computational face of the BCI calibration problem, and it is a central obstacle to deploying subject-specific decoding outside the laboratory.

% --- 2. Background & Key Concepts (~450 words) ---

The machine-learning community has long formalized exactly this kind of problem. The combined algorithm selection and hyperparameter optimization (CASH) problem treats the choice of algorithms at each pipeline stage and their hyperparameters as a single hierarchical search space, to be optimized against cross-validated performance \cite{KotthoffEtAl2019, HutterEtAl2019}. Because pipeline evaluations are expensive and gradients unavailable, the dominant solvers are Bayesian optimization (BO) engines such as SMAC, which fit a surrogate model to past evaluations and select promising configurations via an acquisition function \cite{LindauerEtAl2021, BergstraEtAl2015, AkibaEtAl2019}, together with bandit-based resource allocation \cite{LiEtAl2016, FalknerEtAl2018}. For large conditional spaces, decomposition-based systems partition the search: VolcanoML expresses a CASH problem as a tree-structured execution plan whose building blocks optimize smaller subspaces, and its advantage over monolithic BO grows as the space grows \cite{LiEtAl2021b, LiEtAl2022a}; bandit formulations that progressively discard weak algorithm arms show similar scaling behavior, maintaining accuracy while reducing trial counts by an order of magnitude as the number of candidate algorithms increases \cite{LiEtAl2020a}.

A second body of work addresses the cost of \emph{starting from scratch}. Transfer hyperparameter optimization reuses knowledge from previous tasks to accelerate search on a new one \cite{BaiEtAl2023, Vanschoren2019}. The earliest and most robust mechanism is warm-starting the initial design: selecting the first configurations from those that performed best on similar past datasets, with similarity measured by meta-features \cite{FeurerEtAl2015}. Later work moved the injection point into the surrogate \cite{WistubaGrabocka2021, LiEtAl2022c} or into the search-space definition itself \cite{LiEtAl2022d, PerroneEtAl2019}, and, most recently, into the acquisition function: $\pi$BO multiplies the acquisition by a decaying prior over promising regions, achieving up to 12.5$\times$ faster time-to-accuracy from a purely default-centered prior while provably recovering from misleading priors \cite{HvarfnerEtAl2022}. Robustness mechanisms matter here, because transfer can hurt: multi-task surrogates misestimate cross-task correlation even between closely related tasks \cite{HvarfnerEtAl2026}, and similarity-gated warm-starting with a safe fallback to cold-start search has been proposed as a guard \cite{NomuraEtAl2021}.

The newest knowledge source in this lineage is the large language model (LLM). LLMs have been used to warm-start BO, to act as surrogates and candidate generators \cite{LiuEtAl2024, KristiadiEtAl2024}, to configure software directly \cite{Kannan2023, WangEtAl2025}, and to steer CASH through structured hybrid designs in which LLM-derived context conditions a classical BO engine \cite{XuEtAl2026a, XuEtAl2026b}. Independent reproduction confirms the contextual warm-start effect of LLM-assisted BO with open 70B-parameter models, while also finding that smaller backbones fail to produce usable outputs \cite{RychertEtAl2025}.

% --- 3. SOTA synthesis toward the gap (~700 words) ---

Three findings from these literatures converge on a single design principle. First, \emph{initialization often contributes more than search refinement}: in the original meta-learning warm-start study, the improvement of warm-started SMAC over cold-started SMAC exceeded the improvement of SMAC over random search, and the advantage persisted after fifty evaluations \cite{FeurerEtAl2015}; $\pi$BO reaches the accuracy of iteration fifty of vanilla BO by iteration four given only a well-placed prior \cite{HvarfnerEtAl2022}. Second, \emph{structured search pays precisely when spaces are large and budgets tight}: decomposition and bandit elimination widen their advantage as the number of algorithms and hyperparameters grows \cite{LiEtAl2020a, LiEtAl2021b}. Third, \emph{claimed gains require deflationary controls}: across 114 datasets, competing CASH optimizers were statistically indistinguishable and random search was not reliably worse \cite{ZollerHuber2019}; ensembling without any CASH can outperform CASH-centric frameworks \cite{EricksonEtAl2020}; and a recent budget-matched audit of LLM-advised hyperparameter optimization found that the celebrated ``LLM warm-start'' reduced to a fixed default configuration evaluated before any model call---the LLM added +0.40 percentage points in cross-validation and $-0.01$ on test ($p=0.92$), and classical search seeded with the same default overtook the advisor within twelve evaluations \cite{RodriguesEtAl2026}. Anytime-performance reporting and contamination-free protocols are correspondingly named as open problems by the AutoML benchmarking community \cite{GijsbersEtAl2019, EggenspergerEtAl2021}.

The EEG literature, by contrast, has developed along a disconnected track. Automated optimization of EEG decoding does exist, but it is dominated by evolutionary and swarm-based search over deep architectures \cite{NakisaEtAl2018, DuanEtAl2023, ZhuEtAl2025, AvvalEtAl2025}; Bayesian optimization---the mainstream AutoML engine---appears mainly as a baseline that population-based methods are shown to beat \cite{NakisaEtAl2018}, and hyperparameter studies for deep EEG decoders report large effects whose statistical significance remains largely untested \cite{CooneyEtAl2020}. Meanwhile, the extensive cross-subject EEG transfer literature moves model weights, alignment transforms, and meta-learned initializations across subjects \cite{SchirrmeisterEtAl2017, BerdyshevEtAl2024, HuangEtAl2025, AristimunhaEtAl2025}, with meta-learning libraries automating few-shot adaptation \cite{BerdyshevEtAl2024}. What none of this work transfers is \emph{configuration knowledge}: which pipeline, which filter band, which spatial-filter dimensionality, which classifier and hyperparameters worked for previous subjects. Where per-subject hyperparameter optimization is attempted at all, it is restarted cold for every dataset or subject \cite{ZhangEtAl2024, BerdyshevEtAl2024}. The nearest precedents are classifier recommendation from dataset characteristics \cite{AnarakiEtAl2024} and the implicit reuse of fine-tuning hyperparameters chosen on non-target subjects \cite{BerdyshevEtAl2024}---both stopping short of the search loop itself. On the other side, LLM agents have recently been embedded into BCI toolchains for code generation and analysis, and their authors explicitly call for LLM-driven hyperparameter optimization and architecture search as future work \cite{KapitonovaBall2024}.

% --- 4. Research gap (~350 words) ---

In short, the methodological supply and the application demand are both in place, yet the intersection is empty. Across the 124 studies screened for this review, we found no work that injects transferable knowledge---whether search histories from other subjects or LLM-elicited priors---into Bayesian-optimization-based CASH over a full EEG preprocessing-and-decoding pipeline. This absence is visible from three directions. The CASH systems literature names meta-learned warm-starting as its own next step \cite{LiEtAl2016, LiEtAl2020a, LiEtAl2022a}, but has never treated human subjects as the task family. The transfer-HPO literature has validated its mechanisms on tabular and vision benchmarks \cite{FeurerEtAl2015, HvarfnerEtAl2022, BaiEtAl2023} and its benchmarking community explicitly lacks realistic transfer task families \cite{EggenspergerEtAl2021}, which EEG subjects naturally provide. And the BCI literature, which stands to gain the most---subject-specific pipelines without the twenty-four-hour search---transfers weights but not configurations \cite{BerdyshevEtAl2024, ZhangEtAl2024}. The gap matters practically because calibration cost is the primary deployment obstacle for subject-specific MI-BCI, and methodologically because inter-subject variability, including order-of-magnitude outliers \cite{ZhangEtAl2024}, makes EEG a demanding stress test for the transfer-safety mechanisms the HPO community has proposed \cite{NomuraEtAl2021, HvarfnerEtAl2026}.

% --- 5. Hypothesis & approach (~200 words) ---

To close this gap, we treat each EEG subject as a task in the transfer-HPO sense and extend a decomposition-based CASH framework in the VolcanoML/SMAC lineage \cite{LiEtAl2021b, LindauerEtAl2021} with two interchangeable knowledge sources injected through the same $\pi$BO-style mechanism \cite{HvarfnerEtAl2022}: (a) leave-one-subject-out search histories from other subjects, and (b) priors elicited from a large language model given dataset and subject descriptors. We hypothesize that, under matched evaluation budgets, knowledge-injected warm-starting reaches the cold-start optimum in at most half the pipeline evaluations and improves fixed-budget balanced accuracy across subjects (H1); that zero-shot transfer of source-subjects' configurations outperforms the standard fixed pipeline while similarity gating eliminates negative transfer on outlier subjects (H2); and---importing the deflationary control of \cite{RodriguesEtAl2026} directly into our design---that the warm-start advantage survives a seeded cold-start baseline given the prior's single best configuration as its first evaluation (H3). All experiments follow a budget-matched, anytime-performance protocol with leave-one-subject-out separation between prior learning and evaluation \cite{EggenspergerEtAl2021, GijsbersEtAl2019}, on the PhysioNet EEG Motor Movement/Imagery corpus and BCI Competition IV-2a.

% --- 6. Contributions (~200 words) ---

The main contributions of this work are as follows:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Subjects-as-tasks formulation.} We formulate subject-specific EEG pipeline optimization as a transfer-CASH problem, providing---to our knowledge---the first bridge between decomposition-based AutoML \cite{LiEtAl2021b} and cross-subject BCI adaptation \cite{BerdyshevEtAl2024}.
    \item \textbf{Dual knowledge injection.} We instantiate cross-subject search-history priors and LLM-elicited priors within a single $\pi$BO-style injection mechanism with similarity gating, enabling a like-for-like comparison of structured and language-derived knowledge sources.
    \item \textbf{Deflation-proof evaluation.} We pre-register a budget-matched anytime protocol with random-search, seeded cold-start, and fixed standard-pipeline controls, addressing the known confounds of optimizer indistinguishability \cite{ZollerHuber2019} and default-configuration effects \cite{RodriguesEtAl2026}.
    \item \textbf{A reusable transfer-HPO benchmark.} The per-subject search histories generated on 109 subjects constitute a realistic transfer-HPO task family with anytime curves, released for the AutoML benchmarking community \cite{EggenspergerEtAl2021}.
\end{itemize}
